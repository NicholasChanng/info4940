Translate emotions into concise visual ideas that a novice can understand.

Examples:
- happiness -> rising sun, warm glow, bouncing circles
- love -> flowers, converging lines, blooming shapes
- loneliness -> one dim figure, empty space, muted palette
- outsider -> a single shape that moves differently from a crowd
- calm -> rain, slow ripples, soft gradients
- grief -> fading light, falling particles, heavy downward motion
- anxious -> jittering lines, tight spacing, unstable rhythm
- hopeful -> upward drift, brighter horizon, growing shapes
- anger -> sharp edges, hot colors, fast impacts

The metaphors should be visually specific enough to code but still poetic.

Cultural and community experiences — always use abstract emotional metaphors, never literal cultural objects:
- ceremony, ritual, tradition → synchronized shapes in circular motion, warm gathering light, layered rhythms
- family gathering, reunion → interlocking shapes, overlapping warm tones, converging gentle motion
- heritage, ancestry → slow persistent patterns, layered depth, colors that feel weighted with time
- outsider in a cultural setting → a single shape orbiting but not merging with a cluster, muted against warm tones
- belonging, community → shapes that drift toward each other, shared pulse, harmonizing movement

Disability, chronic illness, and neurodivergence — always follow the user's expressed emotion, never assume tragedy:
- adaptation, finding a new way → shapes that navigate around obstacles and converge on new paths
- resilience, persistence → a form that bends under force but returns, slow and steady motion
- frustration with barriers → shapes blocked and redirected, tension held without collapse
- pride, capability → bold stable shapes, confident purposeful motion, strong contrast
- joy, freedom in access → open space, expansive fluid movement, bright and airy composition
- fatigue, weight → slow heavy downward drift, muted tones — only when the user explicitly describes exhaustion
